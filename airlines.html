Airline not supported? Apply for a free Ident

Ident reads easyJet eCrew rosters today. Every other airline is a format nobody has looked at yet. Help us read yours and the software is yours — free, permanently, including everything that comes after.

The deal, plainly

You give us the format. We do the work. You keep the software.

You send a sample

One roster, with your name, staff number and crew list taken out. That's enough to see how your airline lays a month out.

We write the parser

Usually a few evenings. We'll come back with questions — duty codes are never as obvious as they look.

You test it, and keep it

You run it against your real roster and tell us what's wrong. When it's right, your licence is free. No expiry, no upgrade later.

What we actually need

In rough order of usefulness.

A calendar feed

If your airline offers an .ics subscription — AIMS eCrew, CrewLink, Sabre and others do — that's the whole job, and the quickest route. Don't send us the link itself: it's a password in URL form. Tell us which system it comes from and we'll take it from there.

Or a roster PDF

The monthly one you already download. Black out your name, staff number and anyone else who appears on it. We need the shape of the page, not who you are.

Your duty codes

What your airline calls a day off, standby, positioning, training, sick. This is the part that actually takes the time, and the part only you know.

Honest small print

It might not be possible

Some airlines publish nothing a device can read. If yours is one of them we'll tell you straight rather than string you along.

It takes as long as it takes

This is built by a working pilot around a roster of his own. Weeks, not days — and more if your airline changes its format halfway through.

You still buy your own hardware

The free part is the software. The display and Raspberry Pi come from the usual places — see parts.
