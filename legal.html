Privacy

Last updated 6 November 2026.

The short version

Your roster never reaches us. Ident fetches it from your airline, over your own internet connection, and stores it on your own device. We do not receive it, cannot see it, and operate no service that holds it. There is no account to create. Apart from the licence checks described below, your display sends us nothing unless you switch on flight status or diagnostics reporting. Flight status sends us, on the day you fly, the flight number, departure airport and time, which we pass to Flightradar24 and do not keep; the request carries a pass that does not name your licence or your display. Drive times (Mode S) send us, on the way home, your home location (to about 10 metres; a nearby corner will do instead) and where the drive starts, which we pass to Google Maps and do not keep, under a separate pass that names neither your licence nor your flight. Diagnostics reporting is off by default, is switched on only if you choose Full accuracy on the slider below (or switch it on by itself), never switches itself on, and sends only numbers and fixed words (listed in full in the FAQ).

What your device does

Your display makes outbound connections only to: your roster feed; optionally, as you choose on the slider below, Flightradar24 and Google Maps (both through our server, below) and aviationweather.gov; licence.xpdr.aero, our licence server, once at setup to activate your licence and once a day to check for a software update (and, with flight status on, for the pass that flight status uses); those requests carry your licence and your device's serial number, and nothing else; the same server, only if you switch on flight status, to pass the day's flight number to Flightradar24 (the answer is cached for a minute or two so displays asking about the same flight share it, then gone); the same server, only on Mode S with drive times on, to pass two points, to about 10 metres (where the drive home starts, and your home location, which can be a nearby corner instead), to Google Maps, in a request of its own that carries no flight, no time and nothing naming your licence (the answer, the drive time and, once a drive, the traffic along it, is passed straight back and not kept; Google handles what it receives under the Google Privacy Policy); the same server, only if you switch on diagnostics reporting, to send one small report a day (numbers and fixed words, not tied to your licence or serial number, with no internet address stored); public internet time servers, to set its clock, a request that carries nothing but a request for the time; and, on older software versions, once on first run to download coastline data for the map styles (newer versions include it). Every server your display talks to also sees your home's internet address, as any website you visit does.

What leaves your display, stop by stop

During setup you choose, on one slider, how much your display may send. At the Privacy end only the licence check leaves it; each stop towards Accuracy adds what is described beside it, and you can switch each one on or off by itself later in the control panel. Nothing after the first item is sent until you have chosen and saved a position. These are the same words the display shows.

  1. Full privacy: Your licence key and this display's serial number go to our licence server, licence.xpdr.aero, once when you activate the licence.
  2. Roster and updates: Your display fetches your roster from your own calendar link, and once a day sends your licence key and serial number to licence.xpdr.aero to ask whether there is a new version.
  3. Weather: The code of the airport you are at or flying to goes to aviationweather.gov, the US weather service, at most every 15 minutes.
  4. Flight status: On the day you fly, the flight number, departure airport and time go to our server, licence.xpdr.aero, which asks Flightradar24, the flight data service, for delays, diversions and landing times. Nothing is kept.
  5. Drive times: On Mode S, while you are on your way home, the car park or airport you are driving from and your home location go to our server, licence.xpdr.aero, which asks Google Maps for the drive time in traffic now. No flight, time or licence goes with them, and nothing is kept. Your home location can be a nearby corner or shop rather than your house.
  6. Full accuracy: One small report a day, of counts and fixed words, goes to licence.xpdr.aero, not tied to your licence key or serial number.

Flight status asks from 20 minutes before each flight's scheduled departure until it lands (or, for a flight that is never seen, some hours after it was due), every 5 to 20 minutes. The slider is on Ident for Pi. Ident for E1002 has no slider: it has no weather; flight status, drive times and diagnostics are each off until you turn them on in its settings page, and then send exactly what is described above; and its daily update check stops if you turn off automatic updates.
